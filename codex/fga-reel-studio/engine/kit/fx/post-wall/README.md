# post-wall

Every day the speaker posted, as a calendar heat grid on the wall behind them: one square per day, oldest at the bottom and newest on top like a feed, one row per calendar month. The empty calendar draws up the wall, then the squares land in date order, faster and faster, each behind a one-frame light leading edge, while a mono counter rolls to the total the speaker says; the last square pops in the accent on the word. The speaker's cast shadow falls across the squares that have landed. Squares are flat steps between the pack's dark and light, five or more in the accent; the counter and labels are the pack's mono face. gen.py reads the wall's own tone under the grid: on a dark wall the type is light and more posts are lighter, on a light wall the type is ink, more posts are darker and the accent is the pack's accent on light.

Layer: `behind`, with a CUT_WINDOWS entry over the same window. Reach for it on "I posted every day", "I needed to post more", any streak or body-of-work claim.

Honest by construction: the squares come from the creator's own post dates. A day before the dates reach (`FROM`) is a dim outline, never a zero. `TOTAL` and `DAYS` are what the speaker says on camera; what the dates can't see (other platforms, older history) is spread evenly over the days so the counter lands on `TOTAL`, so only the end value is a claim. With no dates at all it draws a seeded placeholder set and the legend under the grid says PLACEHOLDER DATA: never post that version.

## Needs
- Footage: any for the grid. The speaker's matte (`public/subject.webm`) for the cutout and the cast shadow.
- `words.json` for the cue words, `timeline.json` (the picture cuts decide how it comes in and out).
- The creator's post dates: `DATES` in gen.py (`"YYYY-MM-DD"`, one entry per post: a day posted twice is listed twice) or a JSON in the project (`DATA`, default `work/post_dates.json`): a list like `DATES`, `{"from": "YYYY-MM-DD", "dates": [...]}`, or `{"YYYY-MM-DD": posts}`. An export from their scheduler or analytics tool turns into this easily. `from` is the first day the data covers.
- Optional `work/tracks/track.json`: the squares dim a little away from the head, like the room's light.
- Tripod shot plus the room plate (`room_<FRAME>.npy` from the plate tools, the empty-room stretch) only for the two room pieces below.

## Reuse
Copy the folder to `<project>/fx/<id>/`, fill in the table at the top of gen.py, then `PY fx/<id>/gen.py <project> [<id>]`. It writes fx.html and fx.js and prints what the dates see, the sounds (a tick per frame of fill, rising into the snap) and good frames. Rerun it when the window, the cut, the dates or the numbers change.

- Numbers and dates: `TOTAL`, `DAYS`, `END` (the last day; default the last date), `DATES` / `DATA`, `FROM`.
- Words: `GRID_ON` (the calendar draws), `FILL_ON` (the fill starts), `SNAP_ON` (the last square pops; default the last word of the line). `K` is how hard the fill accelerates. `LABELS` holds DAY, POSTS, NO DATA, PLACEHOLDER DATA.
- Read off the project: the grid is 31 columns centred under the top safe line, its square size from how many months it must fit above `MAX_Y` (out of the captions band); the counter and DAY label sit in the top row's free end, or the grid drops a row to make room when the newest month is nearly full or the speaker's head would hide the DAY label (the labels sit behind the speaker too, so they keep clear of where the matte reaches); the wall's tone picks the type and the ramp; the light falloff centres on the head (track.json). In and out follow the footage: on a picture cut it cuts in (out) with the picture, otherwise one wipe carries it up the wall (`IN` / `OUT`, `WIPE`). gen.py prints the CUT_WINDOWS the window needs; a picture cut inside the window splits them and the wall sits that one frame out (on the cut frame the footage can still show the shot before, and a cutout on it doubles the head).
- `SHADOW` (dx, dy, blur, opacity): the speaker's shadow on the squares, offset away from the room's light; `None` for none. `LIGHT_AT`: a warm light in the room, squares near it warm a little.
- Room pieces, tripod only: `HOLE = (x0, y0, x1, y1)` takes something off the wall for the beat (a frame, a shelf), filled from the wall around it at live levels and played as a clip graded like the footage; draw the box with about 2 x `FEATHER` of plain wall around the thing. `FRONT = [(x0, y0, x1, y1), ...]` keeps what stands in front of the wall (a lamp, a chair back) in front of the squares: inside each box, whatever depth says is nearer than the wall, or whatever looks nothing like the wall round the box's edge (a sconce is too flat for depth to see). Draw each box tight with plain wall all round, only around things in front of the wall (a picture or a door frame on the wall is HOLE material), then check `work/<id>/front.jpg` (the tinted parts stay in front). `FRAME` is the crop (default the shot at the window's start). Both only read the room around their boxes, so they also work on a fallback room (no empty-room stretch, `plate.json` mode "fallback") as long as no box edge crosses the patch the speaker always covered; a HOLE box whose edge crosses bright trim fills lighter than the wall and shows as a box.

**The cast shadow, for any type behind the head**: a copy of `subject.webm` one frame after the window start (`data-start="{{START+0.0333}}" data-media-start="{{SYNC+0.0333}}" data-duration="{{DUR-0.0333}}"`, its own id), offset `left:72px; top:34px`, `filter:brightness(0) blur(32px); opacity:.6`, masked to the bright type it falls on. It only shows on bright things, which is why it reads as lit.

## Phone take
On a stand it works as on a tripod, without the room pieces (`HOLE = None`, `FRONT = []`). Handheld the grid won't stick to the wall: keep it above the head (set `MAX_Y` to the top of the head so the squares shrink to fit above it, or show fewer `DAYS`) and set `SHADOW` dx, dy near 0. Built for the 1080x1920 canvas: a 1:1 cut from the middle of the 9:16 loses the top 420 px, where the counter and the newest rows sit, so set `Y0` to about 470 for a square version.
