# Auditing someone else's text

Read this file first when the job is to judge, assess or clean up a draft someone else wrote. In a clean-up, the hard floor still applies to the returned text. Everything else in this file decides what you keep.

## What to return

Return a verdict (likely human, mixed, likely machine), the tells you found quoted with their line, and the human details you would protect.

## Stacked tells

One flagged word or habit proves little. Call a passage machine-made only at three or more tells in one passage.

## Machine fingerprints

These are near-certain on their own, unlike everything else in this file: chatbot citation junk (contentReference, oaicite, turn0search0, [cite: 1], attached_file, utm_source=chatgpt.com), placeholder brackets left in a finished piece, knowledge-cutoff disclaimers, and links or sources that don't exist. Report them first.

Also flag a sudden change of voice partway through a piece. It usually marks where pasted text starts.

## False positives

None of these on its own counts as evidence of AI:

- clean grammar
- a register that mixes casual and formal
- flat prose that shows no particular tells
- formal vocabulary used properly
- an ordinary greeting or sign-off
- one transition word
- curly quotes
- a single short sentence for emphasis
- repetition that is clearly a choice
- a disclaimer that serves a purpose
- an alternative that deserves weighing
- a common-knowledge claim without a source
- anything inside a quotation or a title
- a wide vocabulary or swapping in synonyms (once a tell, now unreliable)
- text written before late 2022, which is very unlikely to be AI
- a writer who can explain why they chose a word or a cut

## Human details to keep

Protect these during a clean-up, because they carry the writer and are easy to delete by accident:

- odd, specific details
- mixed feelings and tension left unresolved
- references tied to a date or a moment
- word choices the writer could explain if asked
- natural variety in sentence length
- asides and corrections made mid-sentence
