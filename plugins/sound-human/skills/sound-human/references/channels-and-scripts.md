# Channels and spoken scripts

This file holds channel mechanics. The first line, the ending and the ask for each format are in the By format table in SKILL.md, and nowhere else.

## Line breaks

Skim test: read only the first line of every paragraph and the piece should still make sense. On social, mix one-line paragraphs with blocks of two or three sentences, keep each block to about three phone lines (roughly 150 characters), and vary which comes next. A numbered list is fine when the count is in the first line. Emails get up to three single-line paragraphs, and long-form about one per section.

## Channel rules

**Email, one to one.** Plain prose with no markdown, headers, bold or emoji. One link at most, and only when it is the ask. Bullets only for real items. Keep cold emails under 120 words. Never read the recipient's own details back to them ("as the owner of a three-location bakery"). What you know about them shapes the email and stays out of the text. If the writer has met the person before, keep it loose. Write the subject line last, in sentence case, under about 45 characters, saying what the reader gets or what happened, and never as a tease. Sign off the way that person signs a text. When a draft moves into Gmail or another mail app, paste it as plain text (Cmd+Shift+V on a Mac) and make edits after pasting the same way, because text edited after a rich paste can arrive in a different font size, which the recipient sees as a pasted-in draft.

**Newsletters.** Write the subject line and the preview text as a pair, since a phone shows both, and neither one teases. The from-name is a person and the reply-to reaches that person. Links go where the letter points, with the one that matters first. The welcome email says what the reader gets, how often and on which day, and one thing to do now. The unsubscribe line is in the same voice as the rest. Same day, same time, every issue, one header style and no section summaries.

**LinkedIn, Instagram and X.** LinkedIn shows about 210 characters before "more", Instagram about 125 characters of caption, and every blank line uses some of that space. Write to the fold. LinkedIn gets no hashtags and no emoji bullets. Instagram gets three to five lowercase hashtags, grouped at the end. X allows 280 characters per post, and each post in a thread carries one complete idea, because people share them one at a time.

**Chat, DMs and comments.** Anything the user will paste elsewhere gets no formatting. A chat reply keeps whatever formatting the user asked for.

**Long-form.** One header style, no section summaries, and paragraphs that run anywhere from one line to eight. Headers and first sentences form a second piece for the reader who only skims, so write that layer to hold on its own.

**Landing pages and decks.** Each section carries a single idea, with no feature and benefit triplets. The headline says in everyday language what the reader gets.

**Social copy and scripts.** Plain words at about a 6th or 7th grade reading level, pitched at a smart adult.

**Everywhere.** If it fits in a sentence, write the sentence and skip the bullet list.

## Spoken scripts (reels, TikTok, voiceover, video)

- The shape is HOOK, then VALUE (what the viewer should do), then the MENTION inside the teaching, then the one-line CLOSE.
- Hook types to draft across: identity, call-out, result, confession, bold opinion, curiosity, negative, perspective shift, a moment. Specificity breaks ties.
- The hook is the first spoken line, and it also works as on-screen text for people watching with the sound off.
- Every line is one breath, so under about 20 spoken words.
- Real speech hooks clauses together with and, so, but and then, and lets a reaction land in the middle of a sentence.
- The tell is three parallel sentences with the product as the subject ("It logs the call. It tags the lead. It books the follow-up."). Merge those into one running sentence. Short sentences the speaker pauses on ("You stop. Plant your feet. Nothing.") are the beat, so keep them, and mark the punched word in caps.
- Never read a colon list out loud.
- One vague spoken tail such as "the whole thing" or "that kind of stuff" is fine. People talk that way.
- The mention is one sentence, said the way you'd tell a friend about a taco spot.
- A 👇 or similar pointer at a comment close is fine when the writer's samples use emoji.
