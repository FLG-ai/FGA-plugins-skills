# Banned words and phrases

Contents: Tier 1, Tier 2, swap table, banned phrases and frames, sentence skeletons, story clichés, openers, closers.

This file is for the edit pass. The vocabulary was observed through mid-2026 and will drift, and the structural tells file is the durable half.

## Tier 1: never in your own prose

Proper names, direct quotes, code and technical terms are exempt, and so is a word that is the literal, everyday name for the thing (craft beer, the product roadmap).

**On-sight tells:** delve, dive in, tapestry, testament, myriad, plethora, synergy, realm, beacon, paradigm, multifaceted, meticulous(ly), seamless(ly), robust, holistic, pivotal, paramount, nuanced, intricate, comprehensive, revolutionary, unleash, embark, elevate, empower, harness, underscore, foster, illuminate, bolster, showcase, supercharge, revolutionize, leverage (as a verb), utilize, resonate, journey (figurative), game-changing, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, transformative, ever-evolving, fast-paced, in today's.

**Connectors at the start of a sentence:** furthermore, moreover, additionally, in addition, in essence. Write "also", "and", "plus", or leave the connector out. However, therefore, consequently, nonetheless, indeed, thus, notably and importantly are fine alone. Two of them in one passage is a tell.

**Corporate speak:** unpack, deep dive, pivot, aligned, bandwidth (for time), circle back, touch base, game changer, best-in-class, world-class, turnkey, value-add, low-hanging fruit, move the needle, actionable insights, take it to the next level, bridge the gap.

**Fillers:** genuinely, honestly and truly sit in the hard floor in SKILL.md. "Very" and "just" as softeners are out by default, and a voice file that uses them brings them back, since a text with every "just" scrubbed out reads over-edited.

## Tier 2: fine alone, a tell in a stack

crucial, vital, profound, compelling, insightful, stunning, timeless, enduring, renowned, invaluable, optimize, streamline, facilitate, navigate, data-driven, mission-critical, actionable, scalable, dynamic, vibrant, key, insights, transform, craft (as a verb), curate, landscape (figurative), roadmap (figurative), deliverables, alignment, stakeholder, framework (figurative), impact, garner, cultivate, spearhead, underpin, encompass, reimagine, unlock, uncover, shed light on, catalyst, cornerstone, ecosystem, trajectory, endeavor, interplay, bustling, captivating, breathtaking, unparalleled, unprecedented, relentless, unwavering, bespoke, must-have, next-generation, enhance, align with, exemplify, commitment to, diverse array, rich (figurative), valuable, key role, indelible mark, boasts.

Fine alone in the sense a person would use them, a tell in a stack or standing in for a fact ("real impact" where the figure should be). A Tier 2 word the writer's own samples use is not a tell for that writer. If a Tier 2 word is also in the swap table, it's still fine alone. Use the swap only when it's stacked or standing in for a fact.

## Banned phrases and frames

Delete these, then make the point in plain words.

These lists are examples. Anything that makes the same move (a fake reveal, performed honesty, announced drama) is banned too, whatever its wording. The writer's own copy keeps its own phrases (see the voice file).

- **Clearing the throat:** here's the thing, here's the deal, let me explain, let me tell you, the truth is, the reality is, let's be clear, to be clear, I want to talk about something important, here's what I want you to understand, and that brings me to, it's worth noting, it's important to note, it's important to remember, needless to say, it goes without saying, without further ado, in a nutshell, buckle up.
- **Pretend depth:** at its core, at the end of the day, the real question is, in reality, what really matters, fundamentally, the heart of the matter.
- **Staged candor:** Honestly?, Look, Real talk, Let's be real, Let's be honest, trust me, if we're being honest, for real (as emphasis), sincerely (as filler), what you might not expect.
- **Big-reveal hooks:** what nobody tells you, this is the part nobody says out loud, here's the truth no one is talking about, no one is talking about this, no one's saying this out loud, the secret [industry] doesn't want you to know.
- **Story-beat narration:** here's what actually happened, here's where it got [interesting, weird, good], the kind of [X] that makes you [Y] (and its cousins: the type of, the sort of, the [X] that changes how you [Y]), that's when things changed, this is when I realized. Just say what happened.
- **Quiet as decoration:** quietly, quiet ("quietly built", "a quiet shift"). Same for silently, under the radar, without fanfare and slowly but surely, and for "behind the scenes" when it's there for drama and not as the literal place. Cut them unless they're literally true.
- **Fake precision:** that's exactly why, which is exactly why, that's precisely why, precisely (on its own, as agreement), and that's exactly it, and this is why (as a closing reveal), that's the whole point. Connect the two ideas plainly.
- **Hustle framing:** built ("I built a system", "we built this"), built from the ground up, architected or engineered (for ordinary work), crafted (as self-praise), created from scratch (for emphasis). Name the plain verb: wrote, ran, set up, made, started, designed, launched. If the writer's own copy says "built", keep it.
- **Setup questions:** The best part?, The catch?, The scary part?, The wild part?, The result?, Why does this matter?
- **Rebutting objections nobody raised:** this isn't about, I'm not saying, don't get me wrong, this is not to say. An objection stays only when the piece names it somewhere else.
- **Knocking down straw options:** a tempting option would be, one might be tempted to, you might think... but, it would be easy to just.
- **Stock sayings:** X is the currency of Y, the architecture of, the language of, becomes a trap, X is the Y of Z.
- **Puffed-up significance:** serves as a reminder, marks a shift, a key turning point, represents a shift, plays a key role, left an indelible mark, setting the stage for, reflects broader, deeply rooted, focal point, evolving landscape, paving the way for, plays a pivotal role in, stands as a testament to.
- **Name-dropping for status:** as featured in, trusted by industry leaders, with a growing following of, recognized by. A mention can stay if it says what was said and where.
- **Faceless sources:** experts agree, studies show, industry reports suggest, many say, some argue, observers note. Cite the real source, say "I think", or cut it.
- **Canned outlooks and endings:** despite these challenges, looking ahead, continues to thrive, the future looks bright, exciting times lie ahead, a step in the right direction, and never looked back, changed my life.
- **Worn-out filler:** more than ever, like never before, in the first place, it's safe to say, something shifted, that being said, a key takeaway, when it comes to, look no further, let's explore, let's unpack, let's break this down, the possibilities are endless, gone are the days, the lesson here is, what I learned was.
- **Tired openings:** I hope this email finds you well, in today's fast-paced world, in the ever-changing world of, unlock the power of, the world of X.
- **Creator hooks and hype:** stop scrolling, you won't believe, plot twist, this changed everything, will blow your mind, ever wondered, did you know, so get this, hey guys, POV:, story time, you NEED this, run don't walk, obsessed (hype when it's about the writer's own product, a person talking when it's about a thing they love), this is everything, thank me later, I'm not gonna gatekeep.
- **Engagement bait:** like if you agree, tag someone who needs this, agree or disagree?, drop a [emoji] below.
- **Chatbot leftovers:** I hope this helps, Great question, You're absolutely right, Let me know if you have any other questions, Would you like me to.
- **Model disclaimers:** as of my last update, as of my knowledge cutoff, sources on this may be limited, information may have changed, consult a professional (when nobody asked for advice).
- **Self-reports on an edit:** I preserved your voice, I retained the key points, I avoided jargon, I followed all the guidelines, all facts kept. Show the edit and say what changed in plain terms, or say nothing.

## Banned sentence skeletons

- **Antithesis:** "It's not just X, it's Y", "Not only X, but also Y", "X, not Y", "Not X. Not Y. Just Z." Say what the thing is. The hard floor in SKILL.md sets the one exception. A plain comparison that doesn't mirror itself can stay ("from 9 invoices a week down to 2").
- **Mirrored reframes (chiasmus):** "You don't run the inbox. The inbox runs you." "We used to chase leads, now leads chase us." Write the flat, lopsided version with the real claim in it.
- **"Whether you're a X or a Y":** write to one reader.
- **"From X to Y" as decoration,** and fake ranges like "everything from branding to billing to team culture". List the real items or cut the phrase.
- "In a world where...", "That's where X comes in.", "X isn't going anywhere."

## Banned story clichés

couldn't help but feel or wonder, heart pounding, a sense of X washed over, found solace in, the human spirit, from that day forward, little did I know, a stark reminder, a cautionary tale, knew that they had to, felt a newfound sense of purpose, what lay ahead, turn of events, thick with tension, stumbled upon, nestled between, in the heart of, a glimpse into.

## Banned openers

"In today's...", "Picture this:", "Imagine...", "We've all been there", "Let's face it", "Have you ever...", "This essay will explore...", dictionary definitions, and a pile of one-line zingers at the top. Begin partway into things: a particular moment, a name, a figure or a claim.

## Banned closers

"In conclusion", "Overall,", "Ultimately,", "To sum up", "The bottom line is", "So what are you waiting for?", "The choice is yours", "As we move forward, embracing X will be key", stacked commands ("Pick a date. Send the invite. Show up."), and the one-line philosophical bow at the end.
